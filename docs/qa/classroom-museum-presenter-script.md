# Classroom museum visit — presenter script

**Use with:** [classroom-museum-runbook.md](classroom-museum-runbook.md) (setup, rotation, tech).

**Tone:** calm, enthusiastic teacher or museum educator. **Simple words** for mixed elementary/middle groups. Adjust examples up or down by grade.

**Do not** tell students to create school accounts or “sign in with Bluekey.” Staff use the host laptop only.

---

## Timing overview

| Block | Duration | Content |
|-------|----------|---------|
| Welcome + safety | **5–10 min** | Sections 1–2 |
| Station tour + first rotation | **5 min** | Section 3 |
| Rotations | **20–35 min** | Section 4 (repeat per turn) |
| Wrap-up | **5–8 min** | Sections 5–6 |
| Optional Q&A | **5 min** | Section 7 |

---

## 1. Welcome — what is Vellum Rift?

> “Good morning / afternoon! Today we’re visiting a **digital museum** called **Vellum Rift**.
>
> **Vellum** is an old word for parchment — the material ancient books were written on. A **rift** is like an opening. We’re opening a window into **real manuscript pages**, but in **three dimensions**, so you can look closely and explore together.
>
> Some of you will use **VR headsets** — the Meta Quest — and some will use **laptops** in the browser. Everyone is visiting the **same exhibit space** on our classroom network. You’re not on the public internet today; it’s just our room and our server — like a private field trip inside the building.”

*(Point to taped areas and tables.)*

> “We’ll rotate so everyone gets a turn. When you’re **not** in VR or on a laptop, your job is **peer observation** — watch what your classmates notice in the exhibit and be ready to **describe** it in your own words. You might watch them in the headset area, on the **observer screen**, or on a laptop view. Pay attention like a museum buddy taking mental notes.”

---

## 2. Safety — non‑negotiables

> “Before anyone puts on a headset, three safety rules:
>
> **One:** The **tape on the floor is the wall.** Stay **inside** your taped rectangle when you’re in VR. If you drift toward the tape, **stop and step back**.
>
> **Two:** **No running**, no jumping, no reaching for friends. Move **slowly and gently**, like you’re in a library with something fragile.
>
> **Three:** If anything feels wrong — dizzy, stuck, scared, or you can’t find a button — **take the headset off** or raise your hand. An adult will help. In VR, press the **Y button on the left controller** — that’s **Call for help**. A teacher on the laptop will see your name. For the controls list, **raise your left wrist** and look at the **MENU** pad, or ask an adult to show you once.”

*(For elementary, add:)*

> “Only **one person** inside each taped area at a time. Friends and **observers** stay **outside** the tape — **don’t tap** the player or step on the tape to get closer.”

*(For middle school, add:)*

> “Spotters are here to keep you safe, not to scold you. Help each other follow the tape rule.”

*(Hygiene — all grades:)*

> “We use **clean face pads** on the headsets — soft covers that touch your face. **Wait for an adult** to put yours on or swap it before your turn. Don’t share a pad that was on someone else’s face without an adult changing it.”

---

## 3. How stations work

> “We have **three kinds** of spots:
>
> **Quest station** — VR headset. You’ll get **about [5–8 / 8–12] minutes** depending on your grade.
>
> **Laptop station** — same exhibit in the browser. Good for looking at details with a keyboard and mouse.
>
> **Peer observation station** — outside the tape near each Quest area (or at the observer screen). Your job: **watch and listen** for details the player finds. When you hear the timer, you **switch** — Quest → peer observation, peer observation → laptop, laptop → Quest, in the order your teacher shows you.”

*(Hold up timer / rotation chart.)*

> “When I say **‘Switch in one minute,’** finish what you’re doing calmly. When I say **‘Switch,’** hand off the headset to the adult, not to another student.”

---

## 4. Quest onboarding (30–60 seconds per student)

*(Adult fits headset; student seated or standing inside tape.)*

> “I’m going to place the headset on gently. If you wear glasses, tell me now.
>
> Look straight ahead. Use the **controllers** — they’re like remote controls. If you see a **menu** or **events list**, choose our class **exhibit** — it should match the name on the board.
>
> You’ll appear in a **room with manuscripts**. Look left and right slowly. **Do not walk fast.** Your teacher may show the wrist **MENU** once — raise your left wrist and look at it to open **How to Play**. Remember: **left Y** calls for help if you’re stuck. There’s **no log out button in VR** — hand the headset to an adult when your turn ends.
>
> Try three things: **look closely** at a page, **move gently** to see it from another angle, and if your teacher says it’s OK for today, try the **laser** or **pin** tool to point at something interesting — **never** point at people in real life.”

*(Spotter stays at edge of tape.)*

---

## 5. Laptop path (15–20 seconds)

> “On the laptop, open the link or scan the QR your teacher gives you. Type a **nametag** — first name or nickname only.
>
> Click **Enter 3D**. If a popup is blocked, tap **Open 3D** on the same page.
>
> Same rule: **move gently**, explore the manuscript. Laptops use the on-screen **Call for help** button if you’re stuck.”

---

## 6. What to try (during rotations)

*(Repeat briefly between rotations.)*

> “Challenge for this round:
> - Find **one detail** you couldn’t see on a flat picture.
> - Notice how **big** or **small** the writing feels in 3D.
> - If you’re with a partner at laptops, **describe** what you see — don’t shout across the room.”

*(Elementary: one challenge at a time. Middle school: let them pick one.)*

---

## 7. Wrap-up

> “Please **exit** the exhibit — close the tab or hand the headset to an adult.
>
> Today you used **technology historians use** to share fragile books: digital copies you can explore safely. You also practiced **shared space rules** — tape boundaries, taking turns, and asking for help.
>
> **What is one thing** you noticed on the manuscript that surprised you?”

*(Take 2–3 hands from players.)*

> “**Observers** — what did you see **from outside** the tape or on the screen that the person in VR might not have said out loud?”

*(Take 2–3 hands from peer observers.)*

> “Thank you for being careful with the equipment and kind to each other. If your class has a journal, write one sentence about what you’d ask the person who made the manuscript, if you could.”

---

## 8. Optional Q&A prompts

Use if time and attention allow.

- “**Observers:** what detail did you catch by watching someone else explore?”
- “How is exploring in VR **different** from a photo on a slide?”
- “Why do you think we use **tape** instead of letting you walk anywhere?”
- “What would you add to the exhibit so a friend who wasn’t here could understand the book?”
- “What job might use tools like this — museums, libraries, archaeology, game design?”

---

## Reminders for the presenter

- **Chat is off** in the LAN classroom profile — direct Quest students to **left Y (Call for help)** and adults, not typed chat.
- **ControlsGuide** in-headset help still works — demonstrate wrist menu + **L-Y** once on a spare minute.
- Keep **Bluekey / account** language off the mic; only the host laptop uses **Continue as local developer**.
- Celebrate **curiosity**, not scores. No competition to “finish” the manuscript.
